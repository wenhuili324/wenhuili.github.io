<!--
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are a powerful tool for understanding the behavior of molecular systems at the atomic scale. However, one of the primary computational bottlenecks lies in the repeated calculation of interatomic forces, especially for large systems or those requiring high-accuracy force fields. These force evaluations are time-consuming and scale poorly with system size, significantly limiting the achievable simulation timescales.

Machine learning, particularly graph neural networks (GNNs), offers a transformative approach to overcome this limitation. By representing molecular systems as graphs—where atoms are nodes and bonds or interactions define edges—GNNs can efficiently learn the mapping from local environments to atomic energies and forces. Once trained on high-quality data, these models can rapidly predict energies and forces without explicitly computing them from first principles or classical potentials. Moreover, GNNs can be used to construct or approximate neighbor lists dynamically, further accelerating simulations by reducing the overhead associated with neighbor searching. This integration of ML into MD workflows dramatically improves computational efficiency while retaining accuracy, enabling simulations of larger systems and longer timescales that were previously intractable.
